Historical backtest of the frozen pure_alpha_v1.0 strategy with point-in-time lots, listing dates and universe. These are not live or audited results.

Pure Alpha is a long-only trend-continuation strategy on NSE single-stock futures. Signals come from 15-minute candles, every qualifying signal becomes an independently sized position, and positions are held until a stop, target or trailing exit fires, often over several sessions. The research question was whether a simple, fully specified intraday entry could produce a positive risk-adjusted return at portfolio scale once costs, slippage and position-level risk budgets are applied, on the stocks and contract sizes that actually existed at the time.

Where this differs from a pure intraday system. Entries are intraday, but positions are carried overnight by design. The frozen specification has no end-of-day square-off: liquidation near the close was tested and rejected. In this run, positions closed in the entry session lost ₹10.60 Cr in aggregate, while positions carried past it made ₹16.44 Cr.

Point-in-time correction

The frozen study reported ₹12.39 Cr at a Sharpe of 2.02. It had to assume three things it could not observe: that today’s futures lot applied to every historical entry, that today’s 207-name F&O list could be traded across 2016–2026, and that its market-breadth gate could be measured over those same 207 names. This page re-runs the same frozen strategy, with no rule, threshold or cost changed, on the exchange’s own history, one correction at a time.

What moved the result

  • Listing dates, −₹6.70 Cr. 7,305 of the frozen run’s 26,865 positions (27%) were entered on days the stock had no listed futures, and they made ₹6.78 Cr of its ₹12.39 Cr. Most were in stocks NSE added to F&O only after a large rally: ADANIGREEN, PRESTIGE, CDSL and IRFC in November 2024, SOLARINDS in January 2025, RVNL and MAZDOCK in May 2025. Choosing today’s F&O list chooses past winners.
  • Lot sizes, −₹1.16 Cr. The lot listed on each entry day replaces today’s lot. Candle prices are restated for later splits and bonuses, so each historical lot is scaled by a factor measured from NSE’s own price that day (RELIANCE in 2018: 2.10; COFORGE in 2019: 5.0).

  • Breadth, +₹0.05 Cr. Measuring the market-breadth gate over the names listed each day barely changes it.

Methodology

The strategy is fully specified and frozen: every rule below is implemented once and shared by the research engine and the production package. Numeric signal thresholds are withheld from this page. The frozen specification records every value.

  1. Market data

    15-minute spot candles for the 304 stocks that had listed futures at some point in the window, 16.6M bars from 16 Aug 2016 to 11 Aug 2026, from Kite historical data. The listed lot and the listing dates come from the raw NSE F&O bhavcopies.

  2. Signal generation

    Long-only trend continuation. A pullback bar closes back above a medium-term EMA while the shorter EMAs sit above the longer ones. The signal is evaluated only on completed 15-minute closes, after a 600-bar warm-up.

  3. Opportunity selection

    A stock is eligible only on days its futures were listed. A market-breadth gate, measured over the names listed that day, blocks entries unless enough of the market is in trend. Entry-bar range and relative-volume filters remove outsized or illiquid bars. At most one open position is held per stock.

  4. Position sizing

    Whole futures lots of the size listed on the entry day, scaled to the split-adjusted prices: the smaller of what a ₹50,000 risk budget and a ₹10,00,000 margin budget allow, rounded down.

  5. Portfolio construction

    Every qualifying signal is taken. The book is the sum of independently sized positions, with no portfolio-level cap in the research.

  6. Execution

    Two-stage entry. Half the position fills at the signal close. The rest fills only after a confirmation close, and is re-sized at its own fill so the whole position stays inside both budgets. Fills are at the bar close plus 2 bp of slippage per leg.

  7. Risk control

    The position is split into two halves, each with its own volatility-bounded stop. One half books at a fixed multiple of its risk, and the other trails. Stops fire on closes and do not ratchet.

  8. Exit

    A position closes on a stop, target or trailing exit at a 15-minute close. Positions carry overnight, and there is no end-of-day square-off.

Risk management

The controls fall into three groups. Some are enforced inside the backtest. Some were tested and rejected on evidence. The rest exist only in the deployment configuration and were not simulated. They are listed separately so that no control is credited with a result it did not produce.

  • Per-position risk budget, ₹50,000IN BACKTEST0 breaches across 25,123 positions, with risk recomputed leg by leg from both entry prices
  • Per-position margin budget, ₹10 LIN BACKTEST0 breaches. This budget is what makes sizing margin-aware
  • Volatility-bounded stops on each halfIN BACKTESTStops fire on closes, so a gap can overshoot them: 603 positions (2.4%) lost more than 2R, and the worst lost 12.6R
  • One position per stockIN BACKTEST0 overlapping positions
  • End-of-day square-offREJECTEDLiquidating near-stop positions at 15:15 saved about ₹0.7 of loss for every ₹1 of profit it gave up (frozen study)
  • Overnight index hedgeREJECTEDNIFTY puts cost ₹5.30 of premium for every ₹1 of tail loss removed (frozen study)
  • Sector, correlation and volatility capsREJECTEDNo slicing rule saved more than ₹0.33 of tail per ₹1 of profit given up (frozen study)
  • Portfolio exposure capNOT IN BACKTESTPosition count and gross exposure were unbounded, peaking at 160 positions
  • Daily-loss circuit breakerDEPLOYMENT ONLYSet at ₹5 L in the production config, chosen rather than fitted, and not simulated. Its effect on returns is unknown
  • Kill switchDEPLOYMENT ONLYTwo actions: stop new entries while managing open stops, or flatten everything

The three rejected controls were evaluated in the frozen study, on the frozen universe. They have not been re-tested on the point-in-time book.

The market-breadth gate also acts as a regime filter. Every position had closed by 28 Feb 2020, and no new entry was allowed until 7 Apr 2020, so the book was flat through the March 2020 crash. It is a single episode, so it shows the mechanism rather than proving it.

The overnight gap is the strategy’s main risk, and it is also where the return comes from. Positions that survive their first session earn all of the P&L. Positions stopped at the next open are the cost of carrying them.

Research takeaway

  1. Risk-adjusted return: positive, but modest once the universe is honest. ₹5.84 Cr net over 9.9 years at a Sharpe of 1.14, or 8.07% a year on peak margin. Both halves of the sample are profitable (Sharpe 0.73 and 1.46), and so is every point of the trigger grid. The edge per position is thin: profit factor 1.11, a 30.8% win rate, and a losing median position.
  2. The correction is the main result. Replaying today’s F&O list over 2016–2026 more than doubled the backtest, to ₹12.39 Cr and a Sharpe of 2.02. 55% of that P&L came from trading stocks before their futures existed, and most of those stocks were added to F&O because they had rallied.
  3. Drawdown: deep and slow. −₹1.50 Cr, 29.8% of peak margin, from December 2024 and still open when the data ends. The 2017–2020 drawdown, −₹1.46 Cr, took 1,128 calendar days to recover. Four of eleven calendar years lost money.
  4. Scalability: the open question. The book peaked at 160 concurrent positions and ₹5.04 Cr of margin, and used 24% of that on average. Capping each position at ₹15–20 L of notional cuts Sharpe to 0.47–0.82, so the result depends on the larger positions, where market impact has not been measured.
  5. What still stands between this and a live book. Current margin rates rather than historical SPAN; 5.9% of listed stock-days with no candles; spot signals against futures execution, with no basis or roll; 2 bp slippage when costs already take 42% of pre-cost P&L; risk measured on realised P&L; in-sample parameters chosen on the frozen universe; and no live record. The next tests are slippage sensitivity, a roll and basis model, and a walk-forward run on this point-in-time universe.
