A systematic options wheel across the NIFTY 50

A short-put, assignment and covered-call cycle run across the index’s constituents, with the parts that usually get assumed away modelled explicitly: physical delivery, margin, liquidity, corporate actions, dividends and costs.

Research question

The wheel is usually presented as a single-stock income trade: sell a cash-secured put, take delivery if assigned, sell covered calls until the shares are called away. Two things are usually missing. One is a realistic portfolio across many names, sized under margin. The other is honest treatment of the delivery leg, where most of the risk sits.

The question: across the NIFTY 50, with constituents as they stood at each expiry and every leg priced from exchange data, does a ranked, leveraged wheel beat buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted basis? And at what leverage does extra premium stop paying for the stock losses it brings?

Why the window starts in late 2019. NSE moved all stock derivatives to physical settlement by October 2019. Before then, an in-the-money stock put settled in cash, so the assignment and covered-call legs that define the wheel did not exist. The backtest’s first signal is the close of 31 December 2019.

Methodology

An event-driven engine steps through every trading day. Decisions use data up to the close of day t and fill at the close of t+1. No decision reads a later price.

  1. Select. At each monthly expiry, rank current NIFTY 50 members by a trend score, keep names above the threshold, and take the top 20.
  2. Sell puts. Sell the monthly put at or below spot × (1 − 4%) on strikes that traded at least 10 contracts, capped at 10% of the day’s volume.
  3. Settle. At expiry, compare the strike with the final settlement price. An out-of-the-money put expires. An in-the-money put is delivered if cash covers it.
  4. Hold and write calls. Assigned shares collect dividends and carry covered calls at or above spot × (1 + 3%), never below the effective cost basis.
  5. Exit. Shares called away end the cycle. If a stock closes 15% below its price on the day the put was sold, the put is bought back and nothing is delivered.

Every figure is net of brokerage, statutory charges (STT, exchange and SEBI fees, stamp duty, GST) and a per-leg slippage schedule. Idle cash earns the 3-month T-bill, which is also the Sharpe hurdle.

Data

  • Prices: raw NSE F&O and cash-market bhavcopies, covering the legacy format and the UDiFF format that replaced it in July 2024, read directly from the exchange files.
  • Universe: NIFTY 50 membership rebuilt day by day from NSE Indices press releases. Names that were later demoted or delisted stay selectable while they were members.
  • Lot sizes: taken from the contract files at each expiry, including mid-life revisions.
  • Corporate actions: splits, bonuses and rights detected from strike adjustments in the F&O files, demergers handled with explicit values, and cash dividends parsed from NSE announcements and paid on the holding leg.
  • Symbol changes and terminations: renamed symbols are mapped, and contracts that terminated mid-window (for example, HDFC on its merger) settle at the cash-market close.
  • Signals and benchmark: 15-minute and daily bars for the trend score, and the NIFTY 50 Total Return Index from niftyindices.com.

Portfolio construction

The book holds one wheel per name, at most 20 names. The target notional per name is L × NAV / 20, so no name exceeds about 5% of target exposure. Names are allocated in rank order.

  • Covered calls come first. No new put is sold while assigned shares are waiting for a call.
  • Margin decides size. Put margin is modelled as strike × quantity / L, and entries may not push utilisation above 100% of NAV.
  • No borrowing. Cash may never go negative. An in-the-money put that cash cannot cover is realised at intrinsic value rather than delivered.
  • Leverage is a tested parameter. The same rules run at 1×, 2×, 3×, 4× and 5×, and results are compared side by side.

Risk

The wheel is short downside volatility on each name. Risk is concentrated in delivery: an assigned stock that keeps falling carries the full equity loss while the covered call caps the recovery.

The worst episode was the 2024–25 correction, not March 2020. The book was almost entirely in cash when NIFTY made its high on 26 September 2024, and the next day the rules sold a full book of puts, all on the October expiry. That one expiry did most of the damage: six puts were delivered, six more had no cash behind them and were closed at intrinsic value, and the stop-loss bought back three before expiry. NAV fell from ₹6.09 crore to ₹4.74 crore over five expiries while the index fell 15%, and at 3× the drawdown had not recovered by the end of the window.

The lesson is expiry concentration rather than stock selection. When every short put shares one expiry, a single month’s gap becomes a portfolio event. Staggering entries across expiries is the obvious next test.

Validation

The data is checked against independent sources as a set of named, repeatable tests, alongside an engine test suite that includes look-ahead checks. The checks live in the project’s test suite, so they can be rerun whenever the data changes.

  • Constituent historyRebuilt membership matches NSE’s official list, every change has a dated release, and no name is a member before its inclusion date.
  • Trading calendarAll 1,687 cash-market trading days in the research set have a matching F&O file, with trade dates checked inside each file.
  • BhavcopiesEvery cached option row traces back to the raw NSE file for that day, over 21 million rows.
  • Settlement priceThe expiry settlement rule was checked against the expiring future’s settle on 4,625 name-expiry pairs, with a median gap of 0.23 bp. No assignment changes.
  • Option-price adjustmentsStrike and lot adjustments around splits, bonuses and rights are checked against the exchange files, so positions carry across ex-dates on adjusted terms.
  • Lot sizesLot sizes are taken from contract data at each expiry, and a position is never sized with a guessed lot.
  • Rankings coverageEach expiry’s ranking is checked for coverage of current members. Gaps are sized separately rather than filled.
  • Ledger reconciliationCash, positions and P&L reconcile daily, and the engine refuses to fabricate a missing price, lot or expiry.

Where a check found exceptions, such as a small number of contract-listing gaps or a missing day that needed reconstruction, the exception was logged and its effect on trades was measured before results were reported.

Results

Read this with the out-of-sample test. The figures in this section come from settings chosen on this same window. Re-fitted on 2020–2023 and run on 2024 to June 2026, the same settings made 4.83% a year, below the T-bill. See the out-of-sample test.

Over the full window, at the chosen setting of 3× leverage, a 4% put offset, a 3% call offset and a 15% put stop, the wheel compounded at 18.05% a year with a −22.25% maximum drawdown. Over the same window the NIFTY 50 TRI returned 12.23% a year with a −38.27% drawdown.

Moving from 2× to 3× is the last step where extra premium more than pays for extra losses on the delivery leg: each additional rupee of premium costs ₹0.30. From 3× to 4×, it costs ₹1.29. Past 3×, CAGR falls while drawdown keeps growing.

At 3×, the book ran 505 put cycles. 18.8% were assigned, 87% of assigned positions were eventually called away, and delivered stock was held for about 129 days on average.

The case for 2× instead. 2× has the better Sharpe (0.94 against 0.91) and Calmar (0.99 against 0.81), and its drawdown recovered within the window. 3× buys 2.5 more points of CAGR with 6.6 more points of drawdown. That is a preference, not a clear win.

Out of sample

To test the parameter choice, every one of 900 configurations was re-run in full: leverage 1–5×, put and call offsets of 2–7%, and a put stop that was off or set at 10, 12, 15 or 20%. Each was fitted on 2020–2023 and then restarted from cash on 1 January 2024, so no position opened in the fit window carried into the test.

Choosing by return is what failed. Across the 900 configurations, the in-sample CAGR rank had a −0.26 rank correlation with the out-of-sample CAGR rank, so the highest-return settings tended to do worse later. Sharpe and Calmar ranks held up (0.50 and 0.80). The 4% put and 3× leverage had been picked on CAGR. Picked on Sharpe within 3×, the same test selects a 7% put, a 3% call and a 10% stop, which made 9.11% a year out of sample with a Sharpe of 0.49 and a −7.88% drawdown.

The case for the chosen settings. Held fixed through annual test years from 2022, they made 10.21% a year against 8.64% for the NIFTY 50 TRI, ahead of every re-fitted alternative on return. A single regime change, from the 2020–2023 rally to the September 2024 – February 2025 sell-off, can reverse return rankings without any curve-fitting, because leverage and close puts pay in rallies and lose in sell-offs. Why it is weaker: that fixed run was chosen with those years in view, so it is not independent evidence. Its Sharpe (0.39) and drawdown (−28.69%) are worse than the Sharpe-picked alternative’s, and a setting that only works in a regime nobody can call in advance has not been validated.

This test re-uses data that informed the original choice, so it can show the choice is fragile but cannot clear any alternative, including the 7% put. The only clean test left is data after June 2026. A forward test of the 7% put, 3% call, 10% stop setting on data from July 2026 has been pre-registered: one fixed configuration, judged after at least six monthly cycles against the TRI and the T-bill. It has not been run yet.

Limitations

  • Parameters were chosen in-sample, and the out-of-sample test did not support them. Leverage, strike offsets, the stop-loss and the ranking threshold were all set after seeing results on the same window. The walk-forward test above found the chosen settings made 4.83% a year out of sample. The ranking threshold and the 20-name limit were held fixed in that test, so they remain in-sample choices.
  • One path, few events. The window holds 78 monthly expiries, and the worst episode is essentially one expiry. The tails, and so the leverage choice, rest on very few independent observations.
  • Unfunded in-the-money puts are probably flattered. The engine closes them at intrinsic value at expiry. In practice brokers square these off earlier, at prices that still carry time value and spread.
  • Margin is simplified. The model uses strike × quantity / L, not SPAN plus exposure margin. It has no exchange margin hikes in stress and no expiry-week delivery-margin ramp.
  • Cash yield helps at low leverage. Idle cash earns the T-bill rate. A broker pays nothing on idle margin, so earning it requires pledged collateral.
  • Costs are backdated and pre-tax. The 2026 brokerage schedule is applied from 2019, and results exclude capital-gains tax.
  • The trend score is a risk screen, not a return forecast. Its correlation with the next month’s stock return is slightly negative. It decides where puts are sold, not what the stock will do.
  • Residual survivorship. A few members lack intraday bars for part of the window and could not be ranked then. An earlier sensitivity run put the effect at 0.1–1 CAGR point, slightly flattering. It has not been re-run on the final engine.
